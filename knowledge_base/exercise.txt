1. Why exercise is important
Regular exercise is essential because it lowers the risk of all-cause and cardiovascular disease mortality, reduces the risk of chronic conditions (such as hypertension, type-2 diabetes, and site-specific cancers), and improves bone health, cardiorespiratory fitness, cognitive function, and mental health.  
2. Recommended activity
Children & Adolescents (5–17 years): At least an average of 60 minutes per day of moderate- to vigorous-intensity, mostly aerobic physical activity across the week, along with muscle/bone-strengthening activities at least 3 days a week.  
Adults (18–64 years): At least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity (or an equivalent combination) per week, plus major muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week.  
3. Indoor activities
Indoor activities include bodyweight exercises (squats, push-ups, lunges), yoga, high-intensity interval training (HIIT), dancing, active indoor games, or using stationary bikes and treadmills.
4. Outdoor activities
Outdoor activities include brisk walking, running, cycling, swimming, playing sports (soccer, basketball, tennis), hiking, and active commuting (wheeling or cycling).  
5. Stretching
Stretching improves joint flexibility, increases range of motion, and aids in post-workout recovery. Incorporating static stretching after exercise helps keep muscles limber and reduces stiffness.
6. Safety tips
Start small and gradually increase frequency, duration, and intensity over time.  
Warm up before workouts and cool down afterward.
Stay hydrated and wear appropriate footwear or gear.
Pay attention to your body and rest if you feel pain to prevent musculoskeletal injuries.  
7. Benefits
Physical activity improves cardiorespiratory fitness, muscular strength, lipid profiles, blood pressure, glucose control, and sleep quality. It also reduces symptoms of anxiety and depression, enhances memory and executive function, and supports healthy weight maintenance.  
8. Screen time balance
Limit inactive recreational screen time (TV, video games, computers) by replacing it with moderate- to vigorous-intensity movement. Taking short active breaks during prolonged sitting can help mitigate the negative effects of sedentary behavior.  
9. Motivation ideas
Set clear, realistic goals and track progress using devices or fitness apps.
Exercise with a buddy or join group fitness activities/sports.
Mix up your routines to keep things engaging and enjoyable.  
Schedule workouts as non-negotiable appointments in your calendar.
10. Fun exercise tip
Make movement social or play-based: turn on your favorite music for a quick dance party, try gamified fitness apps, or participate in active family/group games like tag or active video games!
