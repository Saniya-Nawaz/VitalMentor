1. What is Fever?
A fever (also known as pyrexia) is a temporary elevation in body temperature, usually above the normal baseline of 98.6°F (37°C). In adults and children, a body temperature reading of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher (measured orally or rectally) is generally considered a fever.
Fever is not a disease itself, but rather a natural immune system response. When the immune system detects a foreign intruder (like a virus or bacteria), the brain's hypothalamus resets the internal thermostat to a higher level to help the body fight off the infection.
2. Common Causes
Fevers are most commonly triggered by the immune system reacting to:
Viral Infections: Colds, flu, COVID-19, dengue, chickenpox, etc.
Bacterial Infections: Strep throat, urinary tract infections (UTIs), pneumonia, ear infections.
Vaccinations: Mild fevers are a common side effect of routine immunizations.
Inflammatory Conditions: Rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, or other autoimmune conditions.
Heat Exhaustion: Overexposure to extreme heat or strenuous exercise in hot weather.
Medications: Certain antibiotics, anti-seizure medications, or high-blood-pressure drugs ("drug fever").
3. Symptoms
Along with an elevated temperature, a person with a fever may experience:
Chills, shivering, or sweating
Body aches, muscle soreness, and joint stiffness
Headache and lightheadedness
Dehydration and dry mouth
Loss of appetite and fatigue/lethargy
Weakness or mild irritability
4. Home Care
Rest: Plenty of bed rest allows the body to dedicate energy toward fighting off the cause of the fever.
Stay Cool: Dress in lightweight, breathable clothing. Use a light blanket if experiencing chills, but avoid bundling up in heavy blankets.
Sponge Baths: Wipe the skin with lukewarm water (never cold or ice water) to gently reduce body heat through evaporation.
Over-the-Counter Medications:
Acetaminophen (Paracetamol) or Ibuprofen can help lower fever and relieve associated body aches.
Note: Never give aspirin to children or teenagers due to the risk of Reye’s syndrome (a severe, potentially fatal condition).
5. Hydration Advice
Fevers increase sweat production and respiration rates, leading to fluid loss and rapid dehydration.
Drink Frequently: Sip liquids continuously throughout the day, even in small amounts if feeling nauseous.
What to Drink: Plain water, electrolyte solutions (ORS), clear broths, diluted fruit juices, or herbal teas.
Signs of Good Hydration: Light-colored urine and regular urination (at least every 4–6 hours).
6. Foods to Eat
Focus on light, easily digestible, and nutrient-dense foods:
Warm Broths & Soups: Chicken soup or vegetable broth supplies fluids and essential minerals.
Bland Carbohydrates: Toast, plain rice, oatmeal, or crackers provide easy energy without upsetting the stomach.
Soft Fruits: Bananas, applesauce, watermelons, and oranges (rich in Vitamin C and high in water content).
Probiotic Foods: Plain yogurt to support digestion, especially if recovering from an illness.
7. Foods to Avoid
Heavy/Greasy Foods: Fried foods, fatty meats, and rich sauces are difficult to digest when body energy is directed elsewhere.
Sugary Foods: Highly processed sweets and sodas can cause inflammation and upset the stomach.
Caffeinated Beverages: Coffee, energy drinks, and strong black teas act as diuretics and can exacerbate dehydration.
Alcohol: Dehydrates the body further and interacts negatively with fever-reducing medications.
Extremely Hard or Spicy Foods: May irritate a sore throat or sensitive digestive tract.
8. When to Consult a Doctor
Seek medical evaluation under the following circumstances:
Infants & Young Children
Infants under 3 months: Any fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher requires immediate medical attention.
Infants 3–6 months: Fever over 102°F (38.9°C) or a persistent mild fever for more than 24 hours.
Child of any age: Fever higher than 104°F (40°C), lethargy, difficulty waking, stiff neck, or fever lasting over 3 days.
Adults
High fever exceeding 103°F (39.4°C) that does not respond to medication.
Fever persisting for more than 3 continuous days.
Red Flag Symptoms:
Stiff neck or severe headache
Difficulty breathing or chest pain
Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or unexplained skin rashes
Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down
9. Prevention
Hand Hygiene: Wash hands frequently with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, or use an alcohol-based hand sanitizer.
Vaccinations: Stay up to date on recommended vaccines (e.g., seasonal flu vaccine).
Respiratory Etiquette: Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or the elbow.
Avoid Close Contact: Limit exposure to individuals with contagious respiratory or viral illnesses.
Healthy Habits: Maintain a balanced diet, prioritize sleep, and stay active to keep the immune system strong.
