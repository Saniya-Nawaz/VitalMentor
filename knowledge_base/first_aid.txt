1. What is First Aid?
First aid refers to the immediate care and initial assistance provided to an injured or suddenly ill person before professional emergency medical help arrives. Its primary goals are to preserve life, prevent the injury or illness from worsening, relieve pain, and promote recovery until advanced medical care is obtained. 
2. Minor Cuts
•	Wash Hands and Protect: Clean your hands thoroughly with soap and water, and put on disposable, latex-free gloves if available. 
•	Rinse the Wound: Gently flush the cut or scrape with cool running water to clear out dirt and debris. Wash the skin surrounding the cut with mild soap, avoiding getting soap directly into the open wound. 
•	Apply Pressure: Apply gentle, direct pressure using a clean, sterile gauze pad until bleeding stops. 
•	Dress the Wound: Apply a thin layer of antibiotic ointment or cream and cover the cut with a sterile adhesive bandage or gauze dressing. 
3. Burns
•	Cool Immediately: For minor (first-degree) burns, cool the burned area under cool running water or apply a clean, cool, damp cloth. Never use ice, ice water, or freezing liquids, as extreme cold can cause further skin tissue damage. 
•	Cover Loosely: Cover the burned area loosely with a sterile, non-stick gauze bandage. 
•	Avoid Harmful Remedies: Do not apply butter, grease, oils, or home salves, and do not break or pop blisters. 
•	Seek Advanced Care: Seek professional medical care for second-degree burns covering a large area or any third-degree burns. 
4. Nosebleeds
•	Position Properly: Sit upright and lean slightly forward. Leaning forward prevents blood from flowing down the back of the throat, which could cause choking or stomach irritation. 
•	Pinch Nostrils: Use your thumb and index finger to firmly pinch both soft sides of the nose shut. 
•	Breathe through Mouth: Breathe through your mouth and maintain continuous pressure for 10 to 15 minutes without releasing to check. 
•	Reapply Pressure: If bleeding continues, pinch the nose again for an additional 10 to 15 minutes. Seek emergency medical help if bleeding does not stop after 30 minutes or following severe facial trauma. 
5. Choking
•	Conscious Adult or Child (Over 1 Year):
o	Encourage coughing if the person can cough forcefully. 
o	If they cannot speak, breathe, or cough, deliver 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. 
o	Perform 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver) by standing behind the person, placing a fist slightly above their navel, grasping it with your other hand, and delivering quick upward thrusts. 
o	Alternate between 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the blockage is dislodged. 
•	Choking Infant (Under 1 Year):
o	Hold the infant facedown on your forearm resting on your thigh, supporting their jaw with head lower than chest. 
o	Give 5 firm back thumps between the shoulder blades. 
o	Turn the infant faceup on your forearm and give 5 chest compressions using two fingers placed just below the nipple line. Alternate until cleared. 
6. Insect Bites and Stings
•	Safety First: Move to a safe location to avoid additional bites or stings. 
•	Remove Stinger: If a stinger is embedded in the skin, scrape or pull it out immediately using a fingernail or clean tweezers. 
•	Clean and Cool: Wash the bite area thoroughly with soap and warm water. Apply a cold pack or ice wrapped in a thin towel for 10 to 20 minutes to reduce pain and swelling. 
•	Symptom Relief: Apply calamine lotion, hydrocortisone cream, or a baking soda paste to lessen itching, and monitor closely for signs of severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis). 
7. Sprains
•	Rest: Rest the injured joint or limb and avoid putting weight on it. 
•	Ice: Apply a cold pack or ice wrapped in a thin towel for 15 to 20 minutes at a time to minimize swelling. 
•	Compression: Wrap the injured area lightly with an elastic compression bandage to support the joint without restricting circulation. 
•	Elevation: Elevate the injured limb above the level of the heart whenever possible to reduce fluid buildup and swelling. 
8. Emergency First Aid Kit
An emergency first aid kit should be stored in an accessible location and checked regularly to replace expired items. Essential items include: 
•	Bandages and Dressings: Sterile gauze pads, roll bandages, adhesive tape, and assorted adhesive bandages. 
•	Protective and Cleaning Tools: Antiseptic wipes, soap, antibiotic ointment, latex-free disposable gloves, CPR face shield, tweezers, and scissors. 
•	Support Supplies: Elastic compression bandages, triangular bandages, cold packs, and splints. 
•	Medications and Documentation: Over-the-counter pain relievers, antihistamines, hydrocortisone cream, emergency contact numbers, and prescription items (such as an epinephrine auto-injector if prescribed). 
9. When to Call Emergency Services
Call emergency medical services (such as 911) immediately if you encounter any of the following critical conditions:
•	Severe Respiratory Distress: Total inability to speak or breathe, severe choking, or stopped breathing. 
•	Anaphylaxis: Symptoms of a severe allergic reaction, such as swelling of the lips, face, or throat, hives, rapid/weak pulse, or vomiting. 
•	Unconsciousness or Trauma: Unresponsiveness, fainting, severe head trauma, or suspected neck or spinal injuries. 
•	Major Bleeding and Severe Burns: Uncontrolled heavy bleeding, nosebleeds lasting longer than 30 minutes, or third-degree/chemical/electrical burns. 
•	Chest Pain or Stroke: Sudden chest pressure, shortness of breath, severe weakness, or facial drooping. 
10. First Aid Safety Tip
•	Check the Scene First: Before approaching a victim, always assess the surroundings to ensure the area is safe for you, the victim, and bystanders. Never put yourself in danger—identifying hazards such as traffic, fire, live electricity, or aggressive insects ensures you do not become a casualty yourself.
