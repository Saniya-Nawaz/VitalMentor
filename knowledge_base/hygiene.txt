1. Why Hygiene is Important
Personal hygiene encompasses individual practices to maintain physical cleanliness, protect health, and prevent disease transmission by removing pathogenic micro-organisms (bacteria, viruses, parasites, fungi).
•	Cost-Effective Public Health: Simple hygiene measures are among the most impactful global health interventions.
•	Infection Reduction: Effective hand hygiene alone reduces diarrhoeal diseases by 30% and acute respiratory infections by 17%.
•	Global Impact: Prevents an estimated 740,000 deaths globally every year.
•	Holistic Benefits: Beyond preventing infections, consistent hygiene supports skin integrity, preserves oral health, promotes self-esteem, reduces social stigma, and protects vulnerable community members.
2. Hand Washing
Handwashing is the single most critical personal hygiene practice for interrupting disease transmission.
Proper Technique: Wet hands with clean, running water, apply plain soap, and rub all surfaces vigorously (palms, back of hands, between fingers, fingertips, thumbs, and wrists) for at least 20 seconds.
Rinse &  Dry: Rinse thoroughly under running water and dry using a clean single-use towel or air dry.
Hand hygiene should routinely be practiced at the five key times identified for community settings: 
1.	Before preparing food. 
2.	Before eating or feeding/breastfeeding others. 
3.	After using the toilet or handling faeces. 
4.	After coughing, sneezing, or blowing your nose. 
5.	When hands are visibly dirty or greasy. 
When soap and water are not readily available and hands are not visibly soiled, an alcohol-based hand rub (ABHR) containing at least 60% alcohol serves as an effective alternative. 
3. Bathing
Regular bathing or showering with clean water and mild soap maintains skin health and bodily cleanliness by removing sweat, dead skin cells, sebum, dirt, and transient pathogens.
•	Daily Body Washing: Focus especially on high-moisture areas—armpits, groin, feet, and under skin folds.
•	Odor Prevention: Prevents unpleasant body odor caused by bacterial breakdown of sweat.
•	Infection Control: Reduces the risk of fungal skin infections (ringworm, athlete&#39;s foot) and bacterial infections (folliculitis).
•	Proper Drying: Dry the body thoroughly with a clean, dry towel to prevent residual moisture from fostering microbial growth.
4. Brushing Teeth
Oral hygiene protects teeth, gums, and oral mucosa from bacterial decay and systemic health complications.
•	Routine: Brush at least twice daily—once in the morning and once before bed—for a minimum of 2 minutes using fluoride toothpaste and a soft-bristled toothbrush. 
•	Plaque Disruption: Mechanically removes dental plaque, a sticky biofilm that converts dietary sugars into enamel-eroding acids.
•	Interdental Cleaning: Use daily flossing or interdental cleaning to remove trapped food and plaque from tight spaces.
•	Disease Prevention: Proper oral care prevents cavities (dental caries), gum inflammation (gingivitis), periodontal disease, tooth loss, and chronic bad breath (halitosis).
5. Nail Care
Fingernails and toenails easily harbor dirt, grease, and microscopic pathogens beneath their edges (subungual space), which can easily enter the body.
•	Maintenance: Keep nails trimmed short, clean, and smooth using clean nail clippers or a file.
•	Washing: Scrub the undersides of fingernails with soap and water during routine handwashing.
•	Habit Avoidance: Avoid biting nails or picking at cuticles to prevent introducing oral bacteria into skin tears.
•	Infection Risk: Prevents painful localized skin infections (paronychia) and systemic contamination.

6.	Clean Clothes
Clothing comes into direct, prolonged contact with the skin, collecting sweat, oils, shed skin cells, and environmental dirt.
•	Microbial Control: Wearing unwashed or damp clothing fosters microbial proliferation, leading to skin irritation, rashes, fungal infections (jock itch), and body odor.
•	Daily Routine: Undergarments and socks should be changed and washed daily.
•	Laundry Care: Wash clothing regularly using laundry detergent and water.
•	Complete Drying: Ensure garments are completely dry before storing or wearing. Drying clothes in direct sunlight provides a natural sanitizing effect via ultraviolet radiation.
7. Food Hygiene
Food hygiene encompasses the conditions and measures necessary to ensure food safety from preparation to consumption, preventing foodborne illnesses such as salmonellosis or cholera. The WHO outlines five core keys to safer food: 
1.	Keep Clean: Wash hands thoroughly before handling food and keep all preparation surfaces, cutting boards, and utensils clean and sanitized. 
2.	Separate Raw and Cooked: Keep raw meat, poultry, and seafood separate from ready-to-eat foods to prevent cross-contamination.
3.	Cook Thoroughly: Cook foods to safe internal temperatures (especially poultry, eggs, and minced meat) to kill harmful foodborne pathogens.
4.	Keep Food at Safe Temperatures: Refrigerate perishable items promptly and do not leave cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
5.	Use Safe Water and Raw Materials: Wash fruits and vegetables with clean, safe water before eating, and select fresh, wholesome ingredients.
8. Germ Prevention
Preventing the spread of infectious disease requires breaking the chain of infection across daily environments. Key germ-prevention practices include: 
•	Respiratory Etiquette: Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue or the bend of your elbow when coughing or sneezing, then immediately dispose of used tissues and wash your hands. 
•	Avoid Facial Touching: Refrain from touching your eyes, nose, and mouth with unwashed hands, as these mucous membranes serve as primary portals of entry for respiratory and gastrointestinal viruses. 
•	Surface Sanitization: Clean and disinfect frequently touched household and workplace surfaces—such as door handles, light switches, countertops, and mobile devices—on a regular basis. 
•	Isolation During Illness: Stay home from work, school, or public gatherings when experiencing contagious symptoms (such as fever, vomiting, or diarrhea) to protect others.
9. Healthy Habits
Personal hygiene is most effective when integrated into a broader foundation of healthy lifestyle habits that support immune function and overall well-being:
•	Nutrition and Hydration: Consume a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole foods, and drink plenty of clean, safe water throughout the day.
•	Rest and Exercise: Prioritize 7 to 9 hours of quality sleep per night and engage in regular physical activity to strengthen immune defenses.
•	Structured Routines: Establish consistent daily wash routines, such as washing hands immediately upon returning home from public spaces or after outdoor activities. 
•	Avoid Sharing Personal Items: Never share personal hygiene tools such as toothbrushes, towels, razors, or nail clippers, which can transmit bloodborne or skin pathogens.
10. Hygiene Tip
•	The "Sing & Scrub" Habit Builder: To ensure you scrub your hands for the full recommended 20 seconds, sing the "Happy Birthday" song (or "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star") twice from start to finish while lathering with soap! Make this routine visual and engaging by placing colorful soap dispensers near every sink in the house—creating a fun, automatic cue that encourages clean hands for the entire family!
