1. Why is sleep important?
Sleep is critical for physiological restoration, memory consolidation, cognitive processing, immune function, mood regulation, and long-term cardiometabolic health.  
2. Recommended sleep hours
Children (6–12 years): 9–12 hours per night.
Teens (13–18 years): 8–10 hours per night.
Adults (18+ years): 7–9 hours per night.
3. Benefits of good sleep
Improved attention, memory, and cognitive performance.  
Enhanced emotional resilience and lower risk of depression/anxiety.  
Stronger immune system function.
Better blood pressure and glucose regulation, supporting cardiovascular health.  
4. Signs of poor sleep
Persistent daytime fatigue or drowsiness.
Difficulty concentrating or brain fog.
Irritability and mood swings.
Taking longer than 30 minutes to fall asleep or waking up frequently throughout the night.
5. Bedtime routine
Set a consistent sleep schedule (going to bed and waking up at the same times daily).
Engage in relaxing activities 30–60 minutes before bed (e.g., reading, gentle stretching, warm bath).
Keep screen devices out of the bedroom.  
6. Sleep environment
Create a cool, quiet, and dark sleeping space. Use comfortable bedding, blackout curtains, and white noise machines or earplugs if external noise is an issue.
7. Things to avoid before bed
Screens: Avoid blue-light-emitting devices (phones, tablets, TVs) at least 1 hour before bed.  
Caffeine & Nicotine: Avoid stimulants in the late afternoon and evening.
Heavy Meals & Alcohol: Avoid large meals or heavy alcohol intake right before going to sleep.
8. Sleep problems
Common sleep problems include insomnia (difficulty falling or staying asleep), sleep apnea (breathing disruptions during sleep), restless leg syndrome, and circadian rhythm sleep disorders.
9. When to consult a doctor
Consult a medical professional if you experience persistent sleep difficulty lasting more than a few weeks, chronic daytime fatigue, loud snoring accompanied by gasping or choking during sleep, or if poor sleep interferes with daily functioning.
10. Fun bedtime tip
Try a "brain dump": spend 5 minutes writing down tomorrow's to-do list or any lingering thoughts in a notebook before turning off the lights to clear your mind and signal to your body that it's time to rest.
